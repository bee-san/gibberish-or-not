# Languages: the contract for language packs

This is what a language pack is, how one is built, and what it must pass to ship. One
agent per language (`gib-lang-<code>`) follows it; gib-lead owns it. If something here
doesn't work for your language, don't work around it: say so (status `NEED_LEAD: ...`)
and gib-lead changes the contract for everyone.

## What a language PR adds

Files only, plus one line in `src/registry.rs`:

```
languages/<code>/
  README.md         sources (URL, version/date, sha256, licence, what was used), sizes,
                    the measured numbers (the tables below), how to rebuild
  build.py          deterministic build from the data cache -> model.bin, build.json
  model.bin         generated (n-grams, alphabet, word table, parameters)
  build.json        generated: inputs with sha256, counts, model size and sha256
  calibration.txt   generated by `cargo run --release -p gib-bench -- fit --lang <code>`
bench/expected/v1/<code>.tsv   generated by `gib-bench gate --write --lang <code>`
tests/lang_<code>.rs           examples that must hold (see "Tests")
src/registry.rs                one line under `// --- <code> ---`:
                               #[cfg(feature = "<code>")]
                               builtin!(v, "<code>");
```

Don't edit shared files: `src/` (apart from that line), `languages/common/`,
`bench/src/`, `bench/scripts/`, `bench/corpus/` (frozen), `Cargo.toml`, `README.md`,
`CHANGELOG.md`, `docs/`. The Cargo feature for every language (`es = []`, ...) is already
declared and on by default; it does nothing until your registry line exists. gib-lead
updates the README, CHANGELOG and docs/benchmark.md when a release ships your language.

If you need a change in shared code (a feature the model lacks for your script, a bug),
set your status to `NEED_LEAD: <what and why, with numbers>` and keep working on the rest.

## The pack format

Write `model.bin` with `languages/common/packlib.py` (`write_model`): it is the only writer,
and its hashes are the ones `src/pack.rs` reads. `languages/en/build.py` is the reference
build. A pack has:

- **Parameters** (`PARM`): `lambda`, the log10 cost of an unknown letter in the word model
  (English: -2.0); `max_word_chars`, the longest word segmentation tries (English: 16);
  `seg_min_letters`, the shortest space-less token it segments (English: 6); optionally
  `rank_offset`, added to the mean n-gram log10 P when packs of one script are ranked
  (default log10 of the alphabet size for dense models, 0 for hashed: set it for a hashed
  model, so that a uniform model of your units scores about 0).
- **Case overrides** (`CASE`, optional): characters lower-cased before Unicode's rules
  (Turkish `I`→`ı`, `İ`→`i`).
- **Alphabet** (`ALPH`) and **dense n-grams** (`NGRM` kind 0), for alphabetic scripts:
  characters map to symbols `0..A`; several characters may share one (fold rare accents
  into their base letter if your alphabet is large); upper case is found by lower-casing.
  Tables `1..order` of `A^k` bytes hold quantised `log10 P(c_k | c_1..c_{k-1})` (`q * 0.04`).
  Any letter outside the alphabet is "foreign": it breaks the n-gram context and counts
  against the text. `packlib.dense_tables` estimates them (interpolated absolute
  discounting). Order 4 over 26 symbols is 457 KB, too much for your budget: use order 3
  (`A^3` bytes) or hashed tables.
- **Hashed n-grams** (`NGRM` kind 1), for large alphabets (Han, kana, Hangul syllables) or
  to fit an order-4 model in the budget: code points as units, per order a table of `2^b`
  u16 slots (8-bit fingerprint, 8-bit quantised log10 P) with a log10 backoff per order;
  `packlib.hashed_tables` builds them.
- **Words** (`WORD`, optional but recommended): up to ~100k words in 15 frequency classes
  (`log10 P` from -1.0 to -8.0 in half steps), hashed (28-bit fingerprints, two-choice
  8-slot buckets). Normalised as the library looks them up: lower case (with the pack's
  overrides), `’` as `'`, no combining marks. Tokens with apostrophes are looked up whole,
  then as their parts (French `l'aube` -> `l` + `aube`: put `l` in the list). Space-less
  text and long tokens are split into words (Viterbi), so a word list helps Chinese and
  Japanese too.

`calibration.txt` is written by `gib-bench fit` (don't edit it by hand): the n-gram gate,
and per length bucket (characters) the logistic weights of the 20 features and the five
anchors that map the logit to the score.

## Data and licences

- **Shipped data** (counts and word lists in `model.bin`) only from public domain, CC0,
  MIT/BSD/Apache-2.0 or CC BY sources, with attribution in your README and the repo's NOTICE
  section for your language (gib-lead adds it at release; list it in your PR).
  Fine: Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR), Project Gutenberg (public domain), Aozora Bunko works whose
  authors died before 1968 (public domain), Wikisource texts that are public domain,
  government texts that are public domain. Not fine for shipping: Wikipedia and Wiktionary
  (CC BY-SA), OpenSubtitles, wordfreq's data (CC BY-SA), Leipzig corpora (non-commercial),
  anything without a clear licence. CC BY-SA and unclear sources may be used for
  evaluation only, and never committed except as frozen evaluation sets with their licence
  noted.
- **Train on the train split's sources only.** Never fit on the corpus's dev or test
  sources: the Gutenberg books `book_split` puts in dev or test, the Tatoeba contributors
  `packlib.tatoeba_split` puts in dev or test (use `packlib.tatoeba_train_texts`), and any
  Wikipedia text. Extra sources you add are train sources by definition (the corpus's
  dev and test slices don't contain them).
- **The data cache** (`$GON_DATA_DIR`, default the program's shared cache) is write-once:
  add files in a directory of your own (`<source>-<code>/`), never change others'. The
  corpus's sources (Tatoeba, Gutenberg, Wikipedia) are already there and listed in
  `bench/sources/sources.json` (shared: don't edit it). Record every file you add (URL, date,
  size, sha256, licence) in `languages/<code>/build.json` (your build script writes it) and
  your README, and append a row to the cache's `SOURCES.md` under `flock` (see
  `_ciphey-common.md`).
- **Deterministic**: `build.py` run twice gives byte-identical `model.bin`; no iteration
  order of sets or dicts may reach the output; randomness only from `random.Random(<fixed
  string>)`. Python standard library only (`$UV venv` if you need a newer Python; no
  network in `build.py` beyond fetching into the cache).

## Budget

- `model.bin` + `calibration.txt` <= **400 KB** (409,600 bytes) per language. English has 1 MB.
- All 13 languages <= **5.5 MB** in the WASM module after `wasm-opt -Oz`
  (`gib-bench sizes --wasm`). English with the code is 1.23 MB today.
- Change a budget only with measurements and gib-lead's agreement.

## How detection works with many languages

For a text, the language-independent pass finds its words, identifiers (neutral) and
shape; the text's majority script picks the candidate packs. Every candidate runs its
n-gram pass; a pack whose n-gram rank is below its calibrated gate drops out. Of the rest,
the **3 best-ranked** (all of them for texts under 12 letters) get the word model and the
classifier; only those within 0.15 of the best rank segment long tokens. The best
calibrated score wins (ties by the logit's margin above the Low anchor), and `is_language`
is that score against the sensitivity's threshold (0.9, 0.75, 0.5).

So a pack can only add acceptances: it never makes another language's text gibberish, but
everything its classifier accepts among the junk is a false positive for the whole
detector. **Your cost is the junk your pack accepts that no other pack did.** That is why
the gates below are about the shared junk false-positive rate.

The rank is the mean log10 P per letter plus log10 of the alphabet size (a uniform model
scores 0), with foreign letters at -3. Packs of one script compete on it: a pack whose
n-gram model fits its own language well ranks first on it, and that is what lets it get
the full scoring.

## The benchmark slice

Corpus v1 (`bench/corpus/v1`, frozen) already has your language's slice: sentences
(Tatoeba, Gutenberg where it has books, Wikipedia in test), paragraphs, phrases, short
phrases, words, upper case and space-less forms (Latin and Cyrillic), sentences with
identifiers, and your language's own junk (wrong Caesar/Vigenère/railfence/columnar keys,
reversed, substitution, encodings, mojibake, code-point shifts for CJK). `gib-bench`
reports it. Don't change the corpus; if your language's slice is wrong (mislabelled
samples, a broken generator), report it to gib-lead with examples: a corrected corpus is v2.

## Workflow

```sh
. /local/home/skerraut/herd-synth3/shared/ciphey/env.sh
python3 languages/<code>/build.py                         # model.bin, build.json
cargo build --release -p gib-bench
cargo run --release -p gib-bench -- fit --lang <code>       # calibration.txt (train + dev)
cargo build --release -p gib-bench                          # the pack is embedded: rebuild
cargo run --release -p gib-bench -- --corpus v1 --split dev # tune on dev, as often as you like
cargo run --release -p gib-bench -- gate                    # the CI gate, with every language
```

`fit` takes `--fpr-scale <x>` (default 1): it multiplies the per-bucket false-positive
targets of `bench/src/fit.rs` for your pack. Your pack's FPR on the shared junk is what
counts against the budget, so expect to need a scale well below 1.

Run the test split **once**, when your model is final, for the numbers in your PR:
`cargo run --release -p gib-bench -- --corpus v1 --split test`.

## Gates (all must pass to ship)

Measured on main plus your pack, with every language on main enabled, on the dev split
unless it says test; put each table in your PR and README.

1. **Recall** of your language's positives (test split, all categories together):
   Low >= 80%, Medium >= 90%, High >= 92%; sentences (category `sentence`) at Medium >= 95%.
2. **Shared junk FPR**: your pack raises the junk false-positive rate of all negatives (all
   languages' junk and the noise) by at most **0.01 percentage points at Low, 0.02 at
   Medium, 0.05 at High** (dev, overall), compared with the same detector without it
   (`gib-bench --langs <every other language>` against `gib-bench`), and `gib-bench gate`
   passes: its per-bucket FPR ceilings are the budget all languages share.
3. **Your own junk**: the FPR of the negatives derived from your language at Medium <= 1%.
4. **Confusion**: of your positives accepted at High, >= 90% are reported as your language;
   no other language's positives are reported as yours more than 3% of the time, and the
   recall of every other shipped language drops by at most 0.2 points.
5. **Latency** (`gib-bench latency`, all languages, same host, back to back with main): p50
   rises by at most 0.3 µs and p99 by at most 3 µs, and with all languages p50 <= 3 µs and
   p99 <= 30 µs for inputs of up to 256 characters.
6. **Size** within the budget; `cargo build --lib --target wasm32-unknown-unknown` works.
7. **CI** green: fmt, clippy, tests (yours included), wasm, gate, size.

A language that can't pass is left out, and its README (in your PR, or gib-lead's) says why,
with the numbers.

## Tests

`tests/lang_<code>.rs`, at least: ten sentences of your language that must be language at
Medium and reported as your language; their upper-case and (for spaced scripts) space-less
forms at High; ten pieces of your language's junk (a Caesar shift, reversed, mojibake, a
wrong transposition key) that must be gibberish at High; one word with your script's case
rules (Turkish `İSTANBUL`, German `STRASSE`). Use `Detector::new().languages(&[Lang::Xx])`
for the language-only checks and `Detector::new()` for the confusion checks.

## The PR

- Title `languages: <Language> (<code>)`; body: what you built and why (model choices you
  compared, on dev), the sources table with licences, the gate tables (dev and the one
  test run), sizes, latency before/after, and every command you ran with its result.
- Your auditor is `audit-gib-lang-<code>` (see `_ciphey-common.md`, "Audits"); land with
  `land.sh` once CI is green on the audited head.
